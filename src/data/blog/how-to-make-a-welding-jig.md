Welding distortion happens because steel expands when it gets hot and contracts as it cools.

If that heating and cooling happens unevenly, the part can pull, twist, bow or move out of position.

## Control the Heat Input

One of the simplest ways to reduce distortion is to avoid putting more heat into the part than necessary.

Shorter welds, proper settings and allowing the material to cool between welds can all help.

## Use a Balanced Welding Sequence

Instead of welding continuously from one end of a long joint to the other, consider using a sequence that spreads heat across the part.

This can help reduce uneven shrinkage.

## Tack and Clamp the Part

Good tack welds and proper fixturing can help keep components in position during fabrication.

However, clamps should support a good welding sequence rather than replace one.

## What We Do in the Workshop

Whenever possible, we test the welding sequence before committing to the final welds.

This is especially useful when working with long plates, thin material or parts where alignment is important.

## What We Learned

- Control heat before trying to correct distortion.
- Tack sequence matters.
- Good fixtures can save a lot of time.
- Test unfamiliar methods on scrap material first.
